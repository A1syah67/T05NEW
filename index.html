What a TV uses, and what the power costs

Every TV sold in Australia carries a star rating and a yearly energy figure in kilowatt-hours (kWh). The first three charts compare 587 TV models by star rating and screen technology. The last one shows how the wholesale price of electricity has changed since 1998.

More stars usually means less energy

Each dot is one TV model. Models with higher star ratings tend to use less energy (the correlation is about −0.47), but the spread is large. A few very big screens use over 1,000 kWh a year. Hover over a dot to see the model.

OLED sets use the most energy overall

Averaged over every screen size, OLED TVs use about 533 kWh a year. LCD TVs use 306. The slice sizes show each technology’s average, so they do not add up to a whole.

At 55 inches the gap is much smaller

Compare only 55-inch TVs and the difference shrinks: LCD and LED are almost equal, and OLED is about 60 kWh higher. In this data OLED sets are larger on average (about 65 inches, against about 50 for LCD), which explains much of the earlier gap.

Electricity prices stepped up after 2015

From 1998 to 2015 the average spot price stayed between about $30 and $65 per megawatt hour. It reached $96 in 2016 and peaked at $144.50 in 2022. Select a name in the key to hide or show a line, and hover over the chart to compare states in any year.

Data: Ex5 CSV files supplied for COS30045 Topic 5 (TV energy labels and electricity spot prices).
